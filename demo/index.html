7Limits and ways to improve

These are the cases the rules get wrong and the data that would fix them.

Where it gets it wrong

Multipath

  • Every short dip is reported as multipath: 305 on eval, most at the row ends with all three tractors dipping together.
  • Without per-satellite data we can't tell row-end shading from true multipath.
  • The row-end dips forced the bad-minute cutoff up to 13 dB, so a long but shallow episode could slip under it.

Overfitting

  • Dev scores 40/40 on only 12 labels.
  • The cutoffs come from each tractor's own normal and from calm dev minutes, never from eval.
  • Every eval call matches a pattern dev already had, so a truly new kind of event would probably be missed or mislabeled.

Two causes at once

  • A jam during a storm is only found as a further step down on every tractor.
  • Overlapping calls lose 0.15 confidence each.
  • When several tests pass, the first in a fixed order wins.
  • Not every combination of two or three causes has been handled or tested: the labels only show a few of them.

Other

  • Jammer on one tractor → equipment fault
  • Base-station outage → three equipment faults
  • Signal fault during a storm → space weather
  • No interference report → jamming confidence stops at 0.85
  • A storm without a magnetic field drop scores lower (eval storm: 0.84)

Ways to improve

  • AGC and noise floor: tells jamming from an antenna fault
  • Per-satellite signal with satellite IDs: tells row-end shading from multipath
  • Wheel odometry or an IMU: a cross-check against spoofing
  • Base-station logs: confirms correction-link faults
  • More labeled examples of multipath and of overlapping causes
  • A jammer footprint sign: the drop grows toward one side of the fleet, as at 18:30 on the eval day
